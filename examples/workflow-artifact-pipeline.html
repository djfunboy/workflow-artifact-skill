5Stage 2 — the decisions in the outline, in order

Stage 2 is short in the skill (S 165–216) but it is where four decisions get made before any HTML exists. Walking them in the order the agent meets them:

Decision 1 — how big is the page?

"Size the document to the subject" (S 169). The branch is sized-to-subject versus padded-to-look-substantial, and the skill names the second as a failure equal to leaving steps out (S 170–172). What enforces it: nothing mechanical — "there are no size gates" (S 172). For this subject the subject is one pipeline with three natural layers (how it runs, what it consumes, where it fails), so: three tabs, unequal.

Decision 2 — does resolution scale down with size?

No, explicitly (S 174–176). A small document still traces to leaf, still names every trigger, still draws both sides of every branch. What enforces it: the floor from section 4. So even a small page gets the full inventory tab.

Decision 3 — are the tabs symmetric?

"Do not force symmetry across tabs" (S 178–179). The pipeline tab carries the weight here; the inventory tab is tables; the honest-state tab is short. What enforces it: the failure-mode box "SYMMETRY FOR ITS OWN SAKE" (S 236–240).

Decision 4 — what shape is each section?

Eight shapes are listed (S 195–204), and the rule is "no shape three times consecutively" (S 193). That is what put a table at section 3 between two diagrams, and a walk-through here between a diagram and a list. Each tab also needs at least one honest-state section (S 206–210) — sections 10, 17 and the verdicts in 14.

Then the agent "produces a visible outline and stops for the user" (S 181). With nobody at the keyboard during the build, the stop became a printed outline and the build continued — a departure recorded in section 10.

6Stage 3 — the constraints BUILD works under

Every item below is a rule the file on your screen had to satisfy, with where it is met.

  • one self-contained file (S 251)no CDN, no stylesheet link, no library. This page has zero external requests — double-click it with the network off.
  • hand-authored inline SVG (S 253)no Mermaid, no D3. Every diagram here is literal <rect> and <path> with a grid comment above it (G 126–140).
  • the look is hard-coded (S 254–257)tokens.css is pasted verbatim as the :root block at the top of this file's <style>; nothing was looked up.
  • green is the page colour (S 258–261)tabs, section numerals, links, the drawer border and the + hover are all --durable. Amber appears only inside SVGs, once per diagram, on the decision point.
  • drawer opens in its own section (S 262)one drawer element, moved before the next <h2> on every click (V 297–307).
  • light and dark, responsive, reduced motion (S 263)dark under both the media guard and [data-theme="dark"]; tables scroll inside .tablewrap; transitions and smooth scroll are off under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • sections written in outline order (S 265–267)this file was appended section by section — diagram, then its caption, then the next — not diagrams first with prose backfilled.
  • doctype · charset · viewport (S 311–317)the three boilerplate lines are the first three lines of the file. Note: the skill states this rule under Stage 5, after BUILD is over — see section 18.

9The rule the whole skill turns on, verbatim

Section 1 of this page exists because of the line below. It is the first thing in SKILL.md after the front-matter, and every later rule — the ~120-word caption target, the expander, the "no wall of text" defect — derives from it.

SKILL.md, lines 10–11 and 13–17

**The deliverable is the DIAGRAMS AND TABLES. Prose is the caption.** A wall of
text is a failure of this skill, no matter how accurate it is.

The value the document creates is **revelation**: the reader looks at it and
says "wait — I thought that step came *after* this one" or "I didn't realize
there were eight files involved." Everything below serves that moment. A
document that only confirms what the reader already believed was not worth
building.

The test it sets is checkable: does the page contain at least one thing the reader did not already believe? For this subject, the candidates are section 1 (five user stops, four before HTML exists) and section 18 (the boilerplate rule lives in the wrong stage).
